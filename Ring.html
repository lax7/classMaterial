Ring Examples

A set R with two operations (+ and ×) is a Ring if it satisfies:

1. Additive Abelian Group: Closure, Associativity, Identity (0), and Inverses (-) for addition. Must be Commutative.
2. Multiplicative Closure: If a, b ∈ R, then (a × b) ∈ R.
3. Multiplicative Associativity: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c).
4. Distributivity: a(b + c) = ab + ac and (a + b)c = ac + bc.
Note: If every non-zero element also has a Multiplicative Inverse, the Ring is called a Field.

1. The Integers (ℤ)

RequirementStatusReasoning
Additive Abelian GroupPassedIntegers have 0 and negatives.
Mult. Closure & Assoc.PassedInteger × Integer = Integer.
Mult. Inverse (Field?)FailedNo multiplicative inverse for numbers like 2.
RESULT: This is a RING.

2. The Real Numbers (ℝ)

RequirementStatusReasoning
Additive Abelian GroupPassedStandard addition properties.
Mult. Closure & Assoc.PassedStandard multiplication properties.
Mult. Inverse (Field?)PassedEvery non-zero number has a reciprocal (1/x).
RESULT: This is a FIELD.

3. Integers Modulo 6 (ℤ₆)

Set: {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5} | Operations: Addition and Multiplication Modulo 6.

RequirementStatusReasoning
Additive Abelian GroupPassedForms a cyclic group under addition with identity 0.
Mult. Closure & Assoc.PassedAny a × b (mod 6) results in {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}.
DistributivityPassedModular arithmetic follows distributive laws.
Mult. Inverse (Field?)Failed2, 3, and 4 do not have multiplicative inverses (you cannot multiply them by anything to get 1 mod 6).
RESULT: This is a RING (with Zero Divisors).

Note on Zero Divisors: In ℤ₆, 2 × 3 = 0. This is a special ring property where the product of two non-zero elements equals zero!
