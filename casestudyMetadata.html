CASE STUDY 2: THE BTK KILLER (DENNIS RADER)

Background: A 30-Year Cold Case

Dennis Rader, known as the BTK (Bind, Torture, Kill) killer, murdered ten people in Wichita, Kansas, between 1974 and 1991. He famously taunted the police with letters but went silent for over a decade. In 2004, he resurfaced, desperate for the media attention he once enjoyed.

The Psychological Trap

Rader’s downfall was his ego and his lack of technological knowledge. He wanted to send larger "packages" of information to the police via digital media rather than paper. He famously asked the police in a letter: "Can I communicate with Floppy [disk] and not be traced to a computer? Be honest."

The Wichita Police Department consulted digital forensic experts and replied with a "coded" ad in the Wichita Eagle: "Rex, it will be OK." This successfully deceived Rader into believing the technology was anonymous.

Digital Forensics: The Smoking Gun

On February 16, 2005, a 1.44MB purple Memorex floppy disk arrived at KSAS-TV. Forensic Investigator Randy Stone utilized EnCase forensic software to image the disk. While the visible file was a simple taunt, Stone examined the unallocated space (space where deleted files reside).

Recovered Artifacts:

The software recovered a deleted Microsoft Word document. By examining the OLE (Object Linking and Embedding) metadata, investigators found:

Metadata FieldRecovered Value
Last Saved By:Dennis
Organization:Christ Lutheran Church
Date:Jan 19, 2005

A simple Google search of "Christ Lutheran Church Wichita Dennis" immediately identified Dennis Rader, the Church Council President. This gave police the probable cause needed to obtain a DNA warrant, leading to his arrest on February 25, 2005.
